9. An exercise as a challenge showing a badly implemented "Building H2O"
   synchronization problem.

Reworked afterwards: no semaphores and no barrier, since the session does
not introduce them; only a mutex and a condition variable.

hydrogen() and oxygen() in the library, each of which has to call the
executable's release_hydrogen() or release_oxygen() once.  The library
keeps counters of the atoms waiting and of the tickets handed out, under
one mutex, with one condition variable.  The skeleton forms a molecule
whether or not the one before has left, and releases the atom after the
unlock.  The reference forms a molecule only when no ticket is left, and
releases with the lock held, calling form() again afterwards.

The threads of the executable share a counter of atoms to bring instead
of each bringing a fixed number: with fixed shares the last hydrogen
thread can be left without a partner, and a correct library hangs.
