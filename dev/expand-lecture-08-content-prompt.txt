We are preparing contents for a class on Operating Systems, part of a 4-years bachelor study program in a technical university.
The class is part of the 2nd year, 1s semester.

We are preparing the materials for lecture-08: Concurrency.
The materials will be of two types:
- Materials to be used / delivered live, under `content/lectures/08-concurrency-live/`
- Materials to be used before and after lecture, with full information, details and explanations, under content/lectures/08-concurrency-full/`

The live / full split follows the similar split used for the labs (the practical part of the class), as in `content/labs/08-concurrency-live/` and `contents/labs/08-concurrency-full/`.

The live delivery of the lecture will take 100 minutes (50 minutes, 10 minutes break, another 50 minutes). In actuality we can expect the delivery to be 40 minutes + 10 minutes break + 40 minutes, because of starting delays and concluding remarks.

Lecture materials, both live and full, are of three types:

- `README.md`: this is the reading material, serving as lesson plan for live and as extensive information for full
- `STORYLINE.md`: this is the planned storyline of the class
- `media/`: stores images and potentially, video, audio or other files as visual support
- `demos/`: practical demonstrations of a concept in action; they are to be demoed live in the live part (only minimal information) and followed individually by students in the full part
- `slides/`: the lectures slides:
   - for full: rich, with details, references / inclusion of diagrams, references for further reading, following the contents of the `README.md`
   - for live: minimalistic, only with diagrams, references to demos and very relevant (yet minimal) information, shouldn't be more than 15 slides per lecture, based on the full part

Current materials live in `content/lectures/08-concurrency-full/`:

- `README.md`: is a skeleton of lecture contents, with pointers for diagrams and demos
- `demos/`: ideas for demos referenced in the README, with the mention that some of them are copies from other session that will require adjustment to fit the current skeleton and spirit of the lectures
- `old-session-synchronization/`: is a previous set of contents for this lecture, when focus on primarily on synchronization, not concurrency; remove this directory after you complete the tasks below
